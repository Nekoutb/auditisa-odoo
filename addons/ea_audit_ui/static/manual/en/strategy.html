The Strategy and risks phase

What the Strategy and risks phase holds, the order to work in, and how your decision to rely on controls shapes the rest of the engagement file.

Who: the team prepares; a partner approves rebuttals of the presumed revenue-fraud risk and risks added after planning, depending on your firm's sign-off policy.

What the phase holds

Click Strategy and risks in the top bar. The ring Strategy and Risk Assessment holds the working papers whose codes start with S: Transactions, flows and walkthroughs (S1), Controls and IT (S2), Combined risk assessment (S3), Specific risk areas (S4), Audit procedure design (S5) and Audit strategy memorandum (S6).

Work in this order: risk register, classes of transactions, IT and close process, combined risk assessment, then procedure design (described in Procedure design).

Deciding to rely on controls

The tasks about testing controls (S2.1 to S2.5, E1.1, E1.2 and E2.1) come onto the engagement file only when your strategy relies on controls. They appear by themselves as soon as you do any one of these:

  • set a class of transactions to the Controls strategy;
  • choose Rely on any assertion of the combined risk assessment;
  • choose one of the two relying strategies for an IT application.

If you stay fully substantive, those tasks stay off the engagement file and you are not asked to complete them.

Good to know The controls tasks (S2.1 to S2.5, E1.1, E1.2 and E2.1) follow your strategy. While you rely on controls they stay on the engagement file. When nothing relies on controls any more, an empty controls task leaves the engagement file, and a task that already holds work stays readable, marked Not applicable with the reason “The strategy no longer relies on controls”. If you rely on controls again, the tasks come back with their earlier work. Both moves are recorded in the activity trail.
