The engagement questionnaire

Answer the engagement questionnaire: AuditISA works out the level of the engagement and the tasks it needs.

Who: any member of the engagement team except a read-only user. The engagement quality reviewer can read the answers but not change them.

Answering the engagement questionnaire

The Engagement profile screen asks 19 questions in seven sections: The entity, The business, IT, Specialists, people and ownership, How the evidence will be obtained, Risk, and Approach and history.

  1. Click a section on the left, or use Next section and Previous.
  2. Click one answer for each question. Some answers open a follow-up question just below (for example, a parent company is asked whether it consolidates).
  3. Watch the panel on the right: it shows the Level so far, marked Provisional, the reasons under Why, and the tasks Added by your answers:.
  4. When all 19 questions are answered, click Conclude. The tasks are put on the engagement file.
  5. Click See the tasks to open the task list.
Tip Answers are saved automatically. You can leave with Back to the engagement and come back later. Until you conclude, the dashboard shows Profile not finished: finish it to get your tasks. with a Finish the profile → link.

There is no "don't know" answer: every question must be answered before you can conclude. If you do not yet know, find out from the client first.

How the answers decide the level

The level is worked out from the answers alone. Nobody can change it by hand. The answers are read in two steps.

LevelWhenWhat the engagement file holds
ComplexAny one sign of complexity: listed (question 1); a bank, insurer or other public-interest entity (question 2); controls testing required by law (question 3); a parent whose components other auditors audit (question 4); more than 3 activities or sites (questions 5 and 6); a major change this year (question 7); any IT point rated High (question 8); specialists needed on the core of the business (question 9).Full ISAs and the full methodology: all task templates.
Non-complexNo sign of complexity, but at least one Very simple condition is not met: for example a regulator receives the accounts, fraud is suspected, an owner is unknown, a going-concern doubt, significant related-party dealings, complex estimates, an approach of Controls reliance (question 18), some work at an interim date, or the use of internal audit's work.A scaled engagement file: core and standard task templates.
Very simpleNo sign of complexity and every Very simple condition met.The shortest engagement file: core task templates only, fully substantive, all at year end.
Good to know For a Non-complex or Very simple engagement whose period starts on or after 15 December 2025, the panel notes that the engagement file may be documented under the ISA for Less Complex Entities.

The planned approach (question 18)

Question 18 offers two options:

  • Substantive only: all the evidence comes from substantive procedures, that is tests of details and analytical procedures. The controls tasks stay off the engagement file, unless the combined risk assessment later relies on controls.
  • Controls reliance: the team tests the controls first and relies on them; the substantive procedures follow and are reduced accordingly. The engagement cannot be Very simple, and concluding puts the controls tasks on the engagement file: S2.1 to S2.5, E1.1, E1.2 and E2.1.

Under either option, tick if they apply Some work at an interim date and Use of internal audit's work. Each one rules out Very simple; the use of internal audit's work also adds S5.3, internal audit.

Good to know With Controls reliance, the Substantive procedures page reminds the team to test the controls first, until the tests of controls (E1.1 and E1.2) are prepared: "This engagement relies on controls: test the controls first. Substantive procedures depend on their result." It is a reminder only: nothing is blocked. An engagement answered before this question changed may show its earlier answer, Something else (earlier answer), with its ticks; it keeps its effect, and its old Rely on controls tick counts as Controls reliance.

Facts decide the tasks, the level decides the depth

Two separate things shape your engagement file. The level decides how deep the work is documented. The facts decide whether a task applies at all, whatever the level. For example:

  • A first-year audit (question 19) adds P1.2, contact with the predecessor auditor.
  • Specialists, tax included (question 9), add S5.1, reliance on experts.
  • Books or key data kept by someone outside the entity (question 14) add S5.2, service organisations.
  • Controls reliance (question 18) adds the controls tasks: S2.1 to S2.5, E1.1, E1.2 and E2.1.
  • Using internal audit's work (question 18) adds S5.3, internal audit.
  • Known or suspected fraud (question 11) keeps the fraud and laws work (P5.1, E3.1, E6.1) on the engagement file.

Answers only ever add tasks. If you change an answer after concluding, the level is worked out again and any new task is added; no task is removed. One exception: changing question 18 from Controls reliance to Substantive only takes the controls tasks off when nothing else relies on controls. A controls task that already holds work stays, marked not applicable with the reason.
