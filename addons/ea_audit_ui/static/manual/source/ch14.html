Findings and the summary of audit differences

This chapter shows you how a matter found during the audit becomes a finding, how a misstatement becomes an adjustment, and how the adjustments reach the summary of audit differences (C1.1) to be reviewed, posted and sent to the client.

Who: preparers raise findings and write adjustments. A reviewer allowed by the firm's sign-off rules posts the adjustments. The quality reviewer reads but does not change them.

One register for the whole file

Every finding, whatever paper it was raised on, builds up in one place: the Findings tool. Open it from the Tools box of a paper that lists it (S4.2, C1.1, C2.2 or C5.1, for example). A finding is one of three kinds:

KindUsed for
Matter for attentionA point the partner or the next year should know about.
Control deficiencyA weakness in internal control, assessed under ISA 265 (likelihood and magnitude).
MisstatementAn error in the figures. It carries an adjustment and goes to the summary of audit differences.

Raising a finding

  • From the Findings tool: click New finding, choose the kind, give it a title and, if you like, the paper it belongs to.
  • From a working paper: on a substantive procedure, a line with an exception or a difference shows Raise a finding. The finding is linked to that paper and appears under Findings on this working paper.

In the Findings tool, click a row to open it on the right. Red marks a significant deficiency, amber a deficiency, and grey another matter or a misstatement. Open its working paper takes you back to where the finding was raised.

Following a finding to its close

A finding moves forward on its own as you fill it in. The filters across the top count the findings at each stage:

StageWhat you record
1 RaisedWhat we found, Why it matters, What we recommend.
2 Assessed (ISA 265)For a control deficiency: Likelihood, Magnitude and the Severity that follows.
3 Discussed with managementManagement's answer.
4 ReportedReported in writing to (for example, the finance director) and the date of the letter.
5 ClosedUnder Close the finding, write how it was resolved, then Mark as closed. A closed finding can be opened again with Reopen the finding and a reason.

Export to Excel gives you the whole register, for a management letter for example.

The adjustment of a misstatement

A misstatement finding has a section called Adjustment to book. Each line uses one of seven headings of the financial statements, not a trial-balance account:

Statement of financial positionIncome statement
Non-current assets, Current assets, Non-current liabilities, Current liabilities, EquityRevenue, Expenditure
  1. For each line, pick the Heading, write a Label and enter the Debit or Credit. Use + Add a line for more.
  2. Choose the Nature: Factual, Judgemental or Projected.
  3. When the debits equal the credits, the editor shows Balanced ✓. Click Save.

Saving sends the adjustment to the summary of audit differences by itself. Equity includes the effect on profit. Open the summary takes you there.

The summary of audit differences

Open it from Summary of audit differences in the Tools box of C1.1. The differences come from the working papers. To add or change one, open its working paper. Click a line to see where it came from. The tabs are 1 Uncorrected, 2 Corrected, 3 Disclosures, 4 Cash flow, 5 Other factors and 6 Tax and conclusion.

  • Brackets mean that the line reduces profit or the balance.
  • Differences below the clearly trivial level are kept aside under Clearly trivial. They are not added to the total, and they are not lost (ISA 450).
  • The Total uncorrected (before tax) and the materiality bar count every adjustment, posted or not.
  • In 6 Tax and conclusion, enter the Tax rate (%) and write the Team conclusion: are the uncorrected differences material, alone or together?

Reviewing and posting a line

  1. Use the Adjustments to review filter to see the lines not posted yet.
  2. If something is wrong, click Review note, write what the preparer should look at, and click Send. The line cannot be posted until the note is cleared with Clear the note.
  3. When you agree with the line, click Post.

The person who prepared an adjustment cannot post it. Nor can the quality reviewer. The firm's sign-off rules for C1.1 decide who may post. To change a posted line, click Unpost first. This is logged, and the line shows changed since posted until it is posted again. A posted line can still be marked as corrected by management.

The client copy

Only posted lines make up the client's list. Click Download the client copy (posted only) to get the Excel file that you send to management with your request to correct. The Posted (client copy) column shows which lines it holds.

The C1.1 working paper

C1.1 Summary of Audit Differences is where you conclude. A card at the top shows the totals from the summary. Its tasks follow ISA 450: accumulate, classify, communicate, obtain the reasons for not correcting, reassess materiality and evaluate individually and in aggregate. C1.1 is never lightened, whatever the profile.
