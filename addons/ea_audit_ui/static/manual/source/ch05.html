Starting a new engagement

This chapter takes you from an empty screen to an audit file with its tasks: you open the engagement, answer the engagement questionnaire, and the tool works out the level of the file and the tasks it needs.

Who: any member of the firm except a read-only user can open an engagement. The engagement partner must be a partner or the firm administrator.

Step 1: open the engagement

  1. On the Dashboard, click + New engagement.
  2. Choose the Client. If it is not in the list, create it first (see Clients).
  3. Type an Engagement name, for example "Statutory audit 2025".
  4. Choose the Nature of the service: Statutory audit or Non-statutory audit. A non-statutory audit leaves out the OHADA tasks that only a statutory auditor performs.
  5. Choose the Engagement partner.
  6. Check the period: Audit period from and to. The Financial year follows the end date.
  7. Choose the Report language, English or French. It is the language of the report, the letters, the legal documents and the exports of this engagement.
  8. Click Create.
Good to know The engagement partner joins the team at once. If you are a staff member, senior or manager, you join the team too, in your own role. A partner who opens a file for another partner is not added to it, and so cannot open it afterwards unless invited.

No question about the client's profile is asked in this window. The questionnaire opens straight after Create.

Step 2: answer the engagement questionnaire

The Engagement profile screen asks 19 questions in seven sections: The entity, The business, IT, Specialists, people and ownership, How the evidence will be obtained, Risk, and Approach and history.

  1. Click a section on the left, or use Next section and Previous.
  2. Click one answer for each question. Some answers open a follow-up question just below (for example, a parent company is asked whether it consolidates).
  3. Watch the panel on the right: it shows the Level so far, marked Provisional, the reasons under Why, and the tasks Added by your answers:.
  4. When all 19 questions are answered, click Conclude. The tasks are put on the file.
  5. Click See the tasks to open the task list.
Tip Answers are saved automatically. You can leave with Back to the engagement and come back later. Until you conclude, the dashboard shows Profile not finished: finish it to get your tasks. with a Finish the profile → link.

There is no "don't know" answer: every question must be answered before you can conclude. If you do not yet know, find out from the client first.

How the answers decide the level

The level is worked out from the answers alone. Nobody can change it by hand, which keeps the scaling of the file consistent across the firm. The answers are read in two steps.

LevelWhenWhat the file holds
ComplexAny one sign of complexity: listed (Q1); a bank, insurer or other public-interest entity (Q2); controls testing required by law (Q3); a parent whose components other auditors audit (Q4); more than 3 activities or sites (Q5, Q6); a major change this year (Q7); any IT point rated High (Q8); specialists needed on the core of the business (Q9).Full ISAs and the full methodology: all task templates.
Non-complexNo sign of complexity, but at least one Very simple condition is not met: for example a regulator receives the accounts, fraud is suspected, an owner is unknown, a going-concern doubt, significant related-party dealings, complex estimates, or an approach other than testing balances at year end.A scaled file: core and standard task templates.
Very simpleNo sign of complexity and every Very simple condition met.The shortest file: core task templates only, fully substantive, all at year end.
Good to know For a Non-complex or Very simple engagement whose period starts on or after 15 December 2025, the panel notes that the file may be documented under the ISA for Less Complex Entities.

Facts decide the tasks, the level decides the depth

Two separate things shape your file. The level decides how deep the work is documented. The facts decide whether a task applies at all, whatever the level. For example:

  • A first-year audit (Q19) adds P1.2, contact with the predecessor auditor.
  • Specialists, tax included (Q9), add S5.1, reliance on experts.
  • Books or key data kept by someone outside the entity (Q14) add S5.2, service organisations.
  • Using internal audit's work (Q18) adds S5.3, internal audit.
  • Known or suspected fraud (Q11) keeps the fraud and laws work (P5.1, E3.1, E6.1) on the file.

Answers only ever add tasks. If you change an answer after concluding, the level is worked out again and any new task is added; no task is removed.

Acceptance and continuance

The new file starts in the Acceptance phase. Its first group of tasks, Acceptance and continuance (P1), covers the decision to take on or keep the client: the acceptance or continuance checklist (P1.1), the predecessor auditor (P1.2, first year only), the preconditions for an audit (P1.3), the engagement letter (P1.4) and the decision on an engagement quality review (P1.5). The independence conclusion sits in P2.1.

The file can move to planning only when P1.1 is complete and concluded "accept", every required acceptance check is answered "yes", the engagement partner has signed P1.1, exactly one engagement partner has accepted, and the review notes on P1 are cleared. Until then, the home page shows Acceptance to sign on the engagement card. Then the engagement partner clicks Advance to planning on the Engagement record.

The engagement partner

Each engagement has exactly one engagement partner, who must be a partner or the firm administrator. The partner gives the partner sign-offs on the key papers and has the last word on the file.

  • To hand the role to another partner, open the Engagement record (gear icon) and click Transfer the partner role. Choose the New engagement partner and the Outgoing partner's new role, then Transfer. Only the engagement partner or the firm administrator can do this.
  • The engagement partner may name a Partner delegate on the same record: another partner on the team who can give the partner sign-offs in their place.
