Planning

This chapter walks you through the Planning phase: what it holds, the tools that do the figures for you (materiality, financial analysis, significant accounts), and the order in which to work so that each step has what it needs.

Who: the whole engagement team prepares; a partner approves materiality.

What the Planning phase holds

Inside an engagement, click Planning in the top bar. The dashboard shows the ring Planning and Risk Identification with its percentage done. Click the ring to see its groups of tasks (the papers whose codes start with P) and how many are finished in each.

Some papers only appear when something on the file calls for them (for example P2.3 for a group audit).

Each paper has a Tools panel on the right. Its buttons open the tool that does the work for that paper. When a tool already holds the answer, the step on the paper says from the tool and is filled for you. You still write your own conclusion on the paper.

The order to work in

The planning tools feed each other. Follow this order and nothing will be empty when you open it:

  1. Import and check the trial balance of the year (and the prior year). Without a valid trial balance, the tools below have no figures.
  2. Look at the Financial analysis and the Analytical procedures (P3.2) to spot unusual movements.
  3. Set and get approval for Materiality (P6.1).
  4. Decide the Significant accounts and their relevant assertions (P6.2). This uses the approved materiality.
  5. Move on to Strategy and risks: the combined risk assessment only lists the accounts marked significant.

You can open every tool from the paper's Tools panel, or from the Engagement tools button (the grid icon in the top bar), which shows all the tools of the engagement as tiles.

Materiality (P6.1)

The Materiality page shows three cards: Overall materiality, Performance materiality and Clearly trivial misstatements. Below them you see the Trial balance bases (the possible benchmarks, worked out from the trial balance), your justification, and the Version history.

  1. Click Generate a version.
  2. Choose the benchmark (Profit before tax, Revenue, Total assets, Equity or Total expenses), check the Base amount and type the Percentage. If last year's file exists, a blue line reminds you of Last year's basis.
  3. Check Performance % (75 by default) and Clearly trivial % (5 by default). If you change the performance percentage, explain why.
  4. Write the Justification. It is required: the tool never fills in a stock sentence for you.
  5. If your percentage is outside the usual range for that benchmark, or your base amount does not match the trial balance, fill Why it departs from the range or the trial balance base.
  6. Click Generate. The new version is saved as a draft.

A partner then clicks Approve the latest version. Only an approved version counts: until the partner approves a new draft, the earlier approved version stays in force, and a yellow banner says so. Approved materiality feeds P6.1, P6.2 and the summary of audit differences (C1.1). Click Export to Excel for a workbook copy.

Good to know If you revise materiality later, the new version needs approval again, and the trail notes that work done under the earlier figure needs review.

Financial analysis and analytical procedures

Financial analysis lists the ratios worked out from the trial balance, grouped under Liquidity, Activity, Profitability, Leverage and Investment. Each row shows the current year, the prior year and the Change. Click the commentary cell (Click to type a commentary) to explain a ratio that moved.

Analytical procedures (P3.2) compares the current year with the prior year by lead schedule. A movement above performance materiality is flagged for you to consider. From there, Lead schedules and commentary opens the detail by account, and Financial analysis opens the ratios.

Tip Write your expectation on P3.2 before you open the figures. The paper asks for it, and it makes the comparison meaningful.

Significant accounts (P6.2)

The Significant accounts page has one row per lead index (for example UA Revenue). For each row you see the Current year, the Prior year, the Variance and Variance %, whether the balance is Above threshold, and, when the general ledger is imported, how many Transactions, Preparers and Reviewers it holds.

  1. At the top, set Flag a significant movement: a percentage and an amount in FCFA, joined by and (both limits passed) or or (either one). The defaults are 10% and 10,000,000 FCFA with and. Flagged rows are counted next to a flag icon. A new account with no prior-year balance counts as above the percentage.
  2. In the Risks column, pick from the engagement's risk register the risks that bear on the account. Any live risk linked locks the row at Significant.
  3. Check the Status the tool proposes (Significant or Not significant). Change it in the cell if your judgement differs.
  4. When you depart from the proposed status, write the Reason. A departure without a reason shows in red.
  5. Tick the relevant assertions in the Relevant assertions column for every significant account.
Good to know The presumed risk of fraud in revenue is linked to revenue (UA) automatically, so revenue stays significant until a partner approves a rebuttal in the risk register. An account excluded here is not worked later, so document an exclusion as carefully as an inclusion.

Use Export to Excel and Import from Excel to work offline. Carried from shows decisions brought over from last year's file.
