Execution

This chapter shows you how to design your procedures, perform them account by account, draw samples, test journal entries and controls, and use the data tools that do part of the work from the client's files.

Who: the team performs the work. Roles that only read (for example the quality reviewer) see every page in read-only mode.

What the phase holds

Click Execution in the top bar. The ring Execution holds Tests of controls (E1), Control updates (E2), Substantive procedures (E4 and E5), Specific procedures (E3, E6) and Registers, opening balances (E6). Journal entry testing (E3.1) sits in the specific procedures group under its own code.

Clicking Substantive procedures opens the Substantive procedures page directly, not a task list. The figure in brackets beside it shows how many accounts are designed in S5.5.

Work in this order: design the procedures, draw the samples, perform the work account by account, then record the conclusions.

Procedure design (S5.5)

Procedure design sets what you will test before you test it. It has two tabs:

  • 1 Design of tests of controls: every control ticked To test on its class of transactions, with its Suggested size (from frequency and reliance) and your Planned size. A smaller planned size asks for a reason.
  • 2 Design of tests of details: one block per significant account, with its combined risk. It is empty until accounts are significant and assessed.

In tab 2, MSP means mandatory substantive procedure: it is ticked and locked for every relevant assertion. ASP means alternative substantive procedure: you tick the ones you choose. The tool highlights the alternatives suggested by the combined risk (red High, amber Moderate, green Low).

  1. Check the mandatory procedures of each account.
  2. Tick the alternatives you need. A High combined risk needs at least one alternative on that assertion before the account counts as designed.
  3. Click Add another procedure for a procedure of your own, and tick the assertions it answers.
  4. Click Draw the sample on a row that needs one: the sampling tool opens already filled with that account and its population.
Good to know You can untick an alternative only until work on it starts. The library covers receivables, payables, inventory, fixed assets, cash, equity and borrowings; for other accounts you add your own procedures.

Substantive procedures

The Substantive procedures page shows one card per significant account: green all done, amber in progress, grey not started, with the number of findings. Only procedures chosen in Procedure design appear.

  1. Click an account. It opens with one tab per procedure, in working order (mandatory first, then alternatives, then your own).
  2. Work through the Steps of the tab and record what you did.
  3. For a problem, click Raise a finding: describe The issue and, if needed, the Adjustment to book. An adjustment goes to the summary of audit differences by itself when you save.
  4. Choose the result (No exception or Exceptions found) and click Mark as done. Click Reopen to change it later.

Some procedures are prepared by the tool from your uploads: the sub-ledger compared with the general ledger, the entries around year end for cut-off, the payments and invoices after year end (search for unrecorded liabilities), customer and supplier confirmations, the fixed asset register against the ledger (K-M1), the vouching of additions and disposals (K-M2) and the test counts and roll-forward of the inventory count (F-M1). When a file is missing, the tab says Data missing and offers Open the uploads.

Lead schedules show the balances by index, with By account, with commentary, Open the general-ledger entries and Extract to Excel.

Sampling

The Sampling page holds every sample of the file, with its reason, so each draw can be explained and repeated.

  1. Click Draw from a dataset (or Draw the sample from Procedure design, which fills it for you).
  2. Choose the Dataset and the method: Random, Systematic, Monetary unit (MUS) or Key items + random. Set the Sample size, and the Key-item threshold, Confidence and Expected misstatement where asked.
  3. To force a size, fill Size typed by hand and Why the size is typed by hand.
  4. Click Draw. Then enter the Audited value of every item.
  5. Click Record the conclusion, or Extend the sample if the projected error is above the tolerable misstatement. A projected error goes to the audit differences (C1.1).

Journal entries (E3.1)

The Journal entries page has four tabs:

  1. 1 Ledger: the ledger tested and its checks. If no ledger is ready, click The ledgers.
  2. 2 Criteria: turn each criterion on or off; the entries it catches are counted at once. Thresholds and own rules sets amounts and your own rules. Click Run the criteria.
  3. 3 Selected entries: open each entry, look at its documents, then clear it or click Raise a finding (Misstatement, Matter for attention or Control deficiency).
  4. 4 Conclusion: the counts cleared and with a problem. Click Record as the S5.4 design to keep the selection as the design, and Export the E3.1 workbook for the file.

Analytics and the data tools

Substantive analytics records an analytical procedure as evidence: lead index, assertion, expectation, actual figure, threshold (a share of performance materiality, set by risk), difference and conclusion. Click Save a procedure to add one.

The Data analytics tile opens the analysers. Each one lists the uploads of its kind, with Import a dataset and Download the template:

AnalyserWhat it holds
Trial balance analyzerUpload, check and map the trial balance.
General ledger analyzerThe journal-entry population, its checks and The thirty analytics.
Accounts receivable analyzer / Accounts payable analyzerOpen items and ageing, Reconcile to the control account, Export the aging.
Supplier statementsStatements against the payables ledger.
Bank analyzerBank statements and outstanding items.
Inventory analyzerThe inventory listing, used for the test counts.
Fixed assets analyzerThe register, used by K-M1 and K-M2.
Payroll analyzerThe payroll register and its agreement to the balance.

Tests of controls (E1.2)

On the Sampling page, the Controls to test section lists every control ticked To test.

  1. Enter the Occurrences in the year on the control, then click Draw the sample.
  2. Test the occurrences drawn. Click Import the test grid (columns: reference, date, description, then one column per attribute with pass, fail or na), or Record a test one at a time.
  3. Read the Operating conclusion.
Careful If a tested control is not effective, the combined risk of the assertions it supports moves to Not rely, and Procedure design suggests more tests of details. Record the deficiency as a finding.
