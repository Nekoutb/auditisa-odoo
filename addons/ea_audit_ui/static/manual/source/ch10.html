Strategy and risks

This chapter shows you how to record the risks, the classes of transactions and their controls, the IT and close-process work, and the combined risk assessment, and how your decision to rely on controls shapes the rest of the file.

Who: the team prepares; a partner approves rebuttals of the presumed revenue-fraud risk and risks added after planning.

What the phase holds

Click Strategy and risks in the top bar. The ring Strategy and Risk Assessment holds the papers whose codes start with S: Transactions, flows and walkthroughs (S1), Controls and IT (S2), Combined risk assessment (S3), Specific risk areas (S4), Audit procedure design (S5) and Audit strategy memorandum (S6).

Work in this order: risk register, classes of transactions, IT and close process, combined risk assessment, then procedure design (described in Execution).

The risk register

The Risk register (S3.1) lists every risk of the engagement: its category (Business, Fraud or Error), its level (Financial statements or Assertion), its inherent rating, whether it is significant, the lead indexes it touches and its status (Identified, Response planned, Response executed, Concluded).

  1. Click Add a risk, describe it and rate its likelihood and magnitude.
  2. Open the risk and click Link to a lead index to say which accounts and assertions it affects. The link also appears in the Risks column of Significant accounts.
  3. Click Link a program step to show how the risk is answered.

Two presumed risks are on every file: Revenue fraud and Management override. Management override cannot be rebutted. To rebut the revenue-fraud risk, a team member clicks Propose a rebuttal and writes why; a partner then clicks Approve the rebuttal or Reject the rebuttal. The person who proposed cannot approve their own proposal, so a second person always checks this judgement (ISA 240).

Good to know A risk added once planning is closed needs a partner to click Approve the addition.

Classes of transactions, what can go wrong and controls

Open Transactions and controls (S1.1 to S2.2). The page shows one card per class of transactions (sales, purchases, payroll and so on): green when complete, amber to finish, red not started. A warning lists any significant account not yet covered by a class.

  1. Click New class of transactions. Give its name, type (Routine, Non-routine or Estimation), the IT applications it uses (separate them with commas) and its Strategy: Controls or Substantive.
  2. Click Assign the class to give it to a team member.
  3. Tab 1 Lead indexes: click Link a lead index and tick the assertions.
  4. Tab 2 What can go wrong: click Add what can go wrong for each risk in the flow, with its assertions.
  5. Tab 3 Controls: click Add a control. Record its owner, type (Manual, IT-dependent manual, Automated), frequency, Reliance (Normal or Heavy / significant risk), design and whether it is implemented. Tick To test for each control you will test. The grid Which control answers which risk links controls to risks: click a cell to link or unlink.
  6. Tab 4 Walkthrough: describe the flow (Initiation, Recording, Processing, Reporting, people, documents, systems), then the walkthrough itself, from Transaction selected to the conclusion.

The panel beside the tabs holds the documents of the class (flowcharts, procedure manuals).

IT applications and the close process

IT applications and IT controls (S2.3 to S2.5) lists every application named on your classes of transactions. For each, record its Layers (application, database, operating system), a strategy and a note:

  • Rely on the IT processes: test the IT general controls
  • Test the automated controls directly, each period
  • Fully substantive: no reliance on the IT controls

When the work is done, click Record the S2.5 conclusion: Support reliance, Do not support reliance or Mixed. This conclusion feeds the combined risk assessment.

Close process (S1.4) is a form in four parts: A. The critical path of the close, B. What can go wrong, C. Changes and communications, D. Strategy and confirmation.

The combined risk assessment

The Combined risk assessment (S3.1) lists only the significant accounts and only their relevant assertions. If the page is empty, it tells you what is missing: a valid trial balance, approved materiality, or accounts marked significant.

  1. For each assertion, choose Inherent risk (Lower or Higher) and Control risk (Rely or Not rely). The tool shows its suggestion and works out the Combined level (Minimal, Low, Moderate, High) and the sampling level.
  2. When you differ from the suggestion, write the Basis / reason.
  3. Tick Show only what is still to assess to see what is left.

Each click saves at once: there is no Save button. Significant risks in the register that are linked to no account are listed at the bottom so none is forgotten.

Careful Rely needs a tested control covering that assertion and IT general controls that support reliance, or a written control risk basis. Once planning is closed, changing an assessment asks for a reason (E6.8).

Deciding to rely on controls

The tasks about testing controls (S2.1 to S2.5, E1.1, E1.2 and E2.1) come onto the file only when your strategy relies on controls. They appear by themselves as soon as you do any one of these:

  • set a class of transactions to the Controls strategy;
  • choose Rely on any assertion of the combined risk assessment;
  • choose one of the two relying strategies for an IT application.

If you stay fully substantive, those tasks stay off the file and you are not asked to complete them.

Good to know The controls tasks (S2.1 to S2.5, E1.1, E1.2 and E2.1) follow your strategy. While you rely on controls they stay on the file. When nothing relies on controls any more, an empty controls task leaves the file, and a task that already holds work stays readable, marked Not applicable with the reason “The strategy no longer relies on controls”. If you rely on controls again, the tasks come back with their earlier work. Both moves are recorded in the activity trail.
